Understand Your Analysis

Learn what One-Way ANOVA, post-hoc tests, F-values, P-values, and mean separation results mean.

01

What is One-Way ANOVA?

One-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) is a statistical test used to determine whether there is a significant difference between the means of three or more independent groups or treatments.

ANOVA compares the variation between groups with the variation within groups to determine whether the observed differences among treatment means are statistically significant.

03

Understanding the Results

F-calculated

The ratio of variation between treatments to variation within treatments. It is used to determine whether differences among treatment means are statistically significant.

F-tabulated

The critical value obtained from the F-distribution based on the selected significance level and degrees of freedom.

P-value

Indicates the probability of observing the obtained differences if there is actually no significant difference among treatment means. A smaller P-value suggests stronger evidence of significant differences.

04

Post-hoc Tests & Superscript Letters

Post-hoc tests are used after One-Way ANOVA to determine which specific treatment means are significantly different from one another. This calculator provides LSD, Tukey HSD, and Bonferroni as post-hoc comparison methods.

The selected post-hoc test is used to perform pairwise comparisons between treatment means based on the chosen significance level.

Superscript letters

Mean separation results may be presented using superscript letters. Treatments sharing at least one superscript letter are not significantly different, while treatments sharing no superscript letters are significantly different.

05

Replication Types Supported

EQUAL REPLICATES

Equal Replication

All treatment groups contain the same number of observations. The calculator supports equal replication and applies the approriate calculations for the selected post-hoc test.

UNEQUAL REPLICATES

Unequal Replication

Treatment groups contain different numbers of observations. The calculator supports unequal replication and performs the appropriate pairwise comparisons based on the selected post-hoc method and the number of observations in the treatments being compared.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How many treatments can I analyze?

You can analyze multiple treatment groups as long as valid replicate values are provided for each group.

Can I use different numbers of replicates?

Yes. The calculator supports both equal and unequal numbers of replicates. The calculations and pairwise comparisons are performed according to the selected post-hoc test.

What does the superscript letter mean?

Superscript letters show statistical grouping in the mean seperation results. Treatments sharing at least one letter are not significantly different, while treatments sharing no letters are significantly different.

What significance level can I use?

The calculator supports common significance levels such as α = 0.05 and α = 0.01 for statistical testing.
